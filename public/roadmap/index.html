Orochi × Onyx — Master Roadmap

A north-star document for the coiling water-dragon: a mesh-native, security-forward, self-hostable real-time community network — and the movement it could start.

Version of this document: 1.0 · Date: 2026-07-06 · Author: strategy synthesis grounded in the live source tree at /home/kain/orochi (daemon, 752 Zig files, ~390k LOC, 6,121 tests) and /home/kain/onyx (SolidJS client, ~43k TS/TSX LOC, 66 components, 80 tests), plus a 19-stream research sweep of the 2024–2026 competitive, protocol, crypto, media, AI, governance, ops, and design landscape.

How to read this. Part I sets the thesis. Part II is the invention catalog — the genuinely new capabilities. Part III is the versioned delivery plan (named milestones, tables, deep dives). Part IV runs each engineering/product track as its own trajectory across those versions. Part V is the design-direction dossier (UI paradigm, color system, backgrounds) the client should adopt. Part VI is moonshots. Part VII is adoption & sustainability. Every audacious idea traces back to a module that already exists in the tree; where something is net-new, it is marked [new].

Part 0 — Executive summary

Orochi is already, quietly, one of the most technically ambitious pieces of communications infrastructure in existence: a 100% clean-room, pure-Zig, no-C-interop IRC/IRCX daemon that has replaced legacy TS6 server-linking with a post-quantum CRDT mesh (Suimyaku transport + Tsumugi X25519+ML-KEM-768 ratchet), ships its own from-scratch TLS 1.3 stack with kTLS kernel offload and PQ-hybrid key exchange, performs session-preserving zero-downtime hot-upgrades (Helix/USR2) that carry live TLS sessions and mesh links across execve, runs services as real server commands (never NickServ/ChanServ pseudo-clients), and is validated by a TigerBeetle-class deterministic-simulation harness (the Deterministic Ocean). Onyx is its first-party client: a SolidJS "Discord-like" web app with an OKLCH theme factory, a local-first IndexedDB history vault with time-travel, E2EE DMs, WebRTC + native-codec group voice/video, presence heatlines, and scheduled events.

No other project in the world combines all of this. Discord has the UX and none of the sovereignty. Matrix has the federation and none of the speed or self-host simplicity. IRC has the openness and none of the modern feel. Slack has the polish and a surveillance business model. Orochi + Onyx is the only stack that can credibly promise all four at once: Discord's comfort, IRC's openness, Signal-grade cryptography, and a network that no company owns — running from a single static binary you can hold in your hand.

This roadmap proposes to turn that latent capability into a movement — call it Sovereign Realtime: the network that remembers you, reaches you, keeps your secrets, and belongs to no one. It sequences the work into four eras and twelve named versions, from near-term-shippable (unify the brand, ship the built-but-not-deployed TLS/kTLS work, pivot the client to a "Time-Native Venue" paradigm, add a Discord importer) through the fortress era (MLS E2EE-everywhere, key transparency, leaderless federation, portable identity) to the venue era (E2EE voice at scale, a WASM plugin/app platform, sovereign local AI) and finally to planetary-scale moonshots (a global anycast media mesh, millions of connections per node, verifiable moderation, and leadership of the next generation of open chat standards).

The through-line of every version is the same discipline that already defines the codebase: every audacious feature rides an open primitive, is proven under simulated failure before it ships, and never trades the user's sovereignty for the platform's growth.

Part I — Vision & differentiation

1.1 The thesis

Sovereign Realtime. Real-time community that is fast (IRC latency, one-second cold load, a 10 MB binary), permanent (local-first memory; the network remembers everything, forever, and so do you, offline), private (end-to-end encryption as the default, not the upsell; post-quantum from the wire up), reachable (push that works with the tab closed), alive (presence you can feel before you join), and nobody's property (a leaderless CRDT mesh, self-hostable from one binary, AGPL, no algorithm, no ads, no account you can be evicted from).

The one-sentence pitch: "Discord comforts, Signal secrets, IRC's openness — on a mesh no company can switch off, from a binary you can docker run in sixty seconds."

1.3 The competitive read (2024–2026)

  • Against Discord (~200–260M MAU, but now shipping mobile ads, Nitro-upsell modals so aggressive they generate their own accessibility-bug backlog, an IPO-driven monetization pivot, a 70K-ID age-verification breach, and country bans that displaced ~40M users): Discord's weaknesses are our identity — no self-hosting, surveillance economics, no protocol, no data ownership, no export path, forced UI changes with no revert. Every Discord-parity feature we add rides an open primitive (IRCv3 draft, IRCX PROP, orochi/* vendor cap). That is the moat.
  • Against Matrix/Element (real government traction — Tchap 600K, BwMessenger 100K+, ~35 countries — but the Foundation nearly went insolvent in 2025, Synapse is heavy, sync latency is chronic, state-resolution is famously complex, and self-hosting is an afternoon of pain): we will never out-federate Matrix; we out-feel it — instant everything, one binary, a client that loads in under a second, and a mesh that is far simpler to reason about than the room-DAG.
  • Against IRC (Ergo, Solanum, InspIRCd v4, UnrealIRCd 6): Ergo is the only real peer (integrated services, always-on, aggressive IRCv3) — and Orochi already exceeds it on four axes no ircd touches: in-protocol voice/video, a PQ CRDT mesh instead of TS6, zero-disconnect hot-upgrades, and a first-party web client + stats + site as one product. The category is shrinking (top IRC networks total ~88–162K concurrent), but its refugees — the FOSS projects that fled Freenode to Libera in 30 days in 2021 — are exactly our beachhead, and they migrate as units.
  • Against Slack/Teams: irrelevant to communities on price and retention (the 2022 free-plan history deletion drove a 40× spike in Zulip imports; the 2024 AI-training scandal burned trust) — but their AI-summary and canvas/list features are worth learning from and beating on privacy.
  • Against the new wave (Bluesky, Revolt/Stoat, Zulip, Campfire, Telegram): Bluesky proves open-protocol social can win 40M users on exogenous waves but bleeds engagement without retention loops; Revolt clones Discord's look but can't federate; Zulip has the best threading model in a dated body; Campfire proves "own your chat" sells but is amnesiac. Nobody in this field owns "beautiful, remembers everything, private by default, and truly yours." That position is empty.

Part II — Paradigm-shift inventions

These are not features on a backlog; they are new capabilities that would change how people think about real-time community software. Each has a codename in the existing mythos, a one-line "why it's a paradigm shift," and the real substrate it grows from. They are delivered across the versions in Part III.

A. Mesh & local-first

  1. Roaming Presence [new] — your presence, identity, and unread-state travel the mesh with you; you are "online" on the network, not on a server. Today ~presence~ gossip already routes DMs to any node; extend the presence lease (suimyaku/state.zig::PresenceLease) into a portable, signed presence object so a user roams between nodes (or reconnects to the nearest geo-node) without re-announcing. Substrate: presence gossip, session_reclaim_mesh.zig, HLC.

  2. CRDT-Native Rooms as Portable Documents [new] — a channel is a mergeable document you can fork, snapshot, replicate offline, and carry between meshes — its members, modes, bans, topic, pins, and props are already delta-CRDTs. Expose a room's CRDT as an exportable, signed artifact: "download this community" produces a file another mesh can import and keep converging. Substrate: channel_crdt.zig, ircx_prop*, burst.zig.

  3. Portable Identity Across Meshes (Sovereign Account) [new] — your account is a keypair, not a row in someone's database. The daemon already derives a sovereign node identity as BLAKE3-160(Ed25519); give users the same treatment: an account is an Ed25519 identity with signed assertions (nick claims, channel authority, oper grants are already signed objects). You can prove who you are on any mesh that will admit you — the anti-deplatforming primitive. Substrate: oper_cred_share.zig, meshpass.zig, signed_object.zig, certfp/SASL EXTERNAL, WebAuthn.

  4. Offline-First Everything [new] — compose, read, search, and moderate with the network unplugged; sync on reconnect. The client vault + outbox already do half of this; the mesh's CRDT semantics make the server side offline-tolerant too (a node that rejoins after a partition converges cleanly). Close the loop: offline moderation actions queue as signed ops and merge on heal. Substrate: vault/outbox, delta-CRDT merge, netsplit_batch.

  5. Time-Native History (the network that remembers) — history is navigable space, not a scroll. Content-addressed history (the planned "Lotus" store), the client's ?at= time-travel, and the presence heatline become one coherent primitive: scrub a room's timeline, share a moment as a link, get a rendered "what happened since you left" digest. Substrate: vault time-travel, chanstats hours[], CHATHISTORY, planned Lotus content-addressed log.

B. Security, crypto & trust

  1. E2EE Everywhere via MLS (Kintsugi ratchet) — group end-to-end encryption for DMs, rooms, and calls, post-quantum-ready, with the server as delivery-service-never-member. The crypto is already in the tree — treekem.zig (MLS-style tree), mls_keyschedule.zig (RFC 9420 key schedule), sframe.zig (RFC 9605 media frames), ratchet.zig, hpke.zig. Wire them into a Discord-DAVE-grade group ratchet, but correct DAVE's known flaws (authenticate the clear-range metadata in AAD, use a key-committing construction, no downgrade/passthrough mode because we control all clients). This is the headline: the only self-hostable Discord-class platform with audited-design E2EE voice.

  2. Key Transparency (Verifiable Identity) [new] — nobody — not even your server operator — can silently swap your keys. Append account key bindings to a Merkle transparency log (the substrate already has merkle_tree.zig, merkle_mountain_range.zig, sparse_merkle.zig, and vrf.zig for privacy-preserving lookups). Clients audit their own key history; this is the WhatsApp/iMessage key-transparency pattern, self-hostable, defeating the exact "AI-agent needs plaintext" and "operator swaps your key" attacks. Substrate: Merkle trees + VRF, signed account assertions.

  3. Capability-Based Access (Object-Capability Mesh) — authority is an unforgeable, attenuable token, not an ambient role. MeshPass already encodes {roles, allowed_frame_families, max_fanout, media_rights, revocation_epoch} as an Ed25519-signed capability. Generalize this to user/plugin/bot authority (UCAN/Biscuit-style: any holder can mint a narrower token, never a wider one) so "install rights on #chan with capability subset," "this bot may post to #x until T," and cross-mesh delegation all compose without a central auth server. Substrate: meshpass.zig, scoped_access.zig, oper_cred_share.zig.

  4. Verifiable Moderation (ProofMark) [new] — every ban, mute, and forced mode is a signed, auditable proof — moderation without a black box. Every WARD, KILL, access change, and redaction emits a signed record (actor authority, policy version, target, reason hash, expiry) that peers verify before applying. Users can receive a redacted proof id; opers inspect the full chain via the Event Spine's audit trail. Substrate: warden.zig, Event Spine audit_trail.zig, signed_delta.zig.

  5. Sovereign-Grade PQ + ECH (metadata privacy) — not just what you say, but who you talk to, hidden. Finish the TLS roadmap's Phase 5 ECH (Encrypted Client Hello) so even the SNI is private, and extend PQ signatures (ML-DSA/SLH-DSA) as CAs issue them. Substrate: sni.zig, ech.zig (codec exists), TLS roadmap.

C. Performance & scale

  1. Every Node Is the Whole Network (Geo-Mesh) — no hub-and-spoke tree; run three nodes in three regions and each is a full, netsplit-tolerant replica. The CRDT mesh already replicates full state; add GeoDNS/latency routing and co-locate media relay/TURN on every node (the Cloudflare "run everything everywhere" pattern, scaled down). No self-hostable chat platform ships integrated cross-node media.
  2. Mesh-Cascaded SFU [new] — a publisher's stream crosses each s2s link once, then fans out locally — Jitsi-Octo/LiveKit-mesh cascading over the existing authenticated Tsumugi links, turning the mesh from a chat feature into a media-scaling feature.
  3. kTLS-Everywhere + io_uring Frontier — the kernel encrypts; userspace never copies plaintext. kTLS TX+RX already landed (gated); push it to the wss media/upload data-plane, add zero-copy RX (iou-zcrx), multishot recv + provided buffer rings + DEFER_TASKRUN per sharded reactor — the path to a credible "1M+ connections, 100–500K active per node" claim. Substrate: ktls.zig, Ringlane io/ring.zig, sharded reactor fabric.

D. Client & UX

  1. The Time-Native Venue — stop designing a place to post; design a place to return to. A reading-first, command-navigated (Cmd-K with a time grammar: at: yesterday 21:00), calm-by-default client where catching up is the main event, presence is ambient, and time is a first-class navigable dimension. (Full spec: Part V.)
  2. Rooms as Places (Ambient Presence) — each room is inhabited, not a document with a badge — a per-room atmosphere (theme-reactive background), presence heatline, visible voice "corner," and quiet reactions (Campfire-style Boosts that notify no one). Substrate: PresenceHeatline.tsx, PresenceRibbon.tsx, animated background engine.
  3. Programmable / Scriptable Client [new] — the client is an extensible surface — a Block-Kit-lite structured-message layer (buttons/selects/modals as IRCv3-tag payloads the SolidJS client renders natively, degrading to text for legacy clients), plus a later Figma-style QuickJS-in-WASM sandbox for client extensions.
  4. Sovereign Local AI (Amanogawa) — your AI runs on your hardware, over your data; the cloud never sees a token. Catch-up digests, semantic search over the E2EE vault (client-side embeddings the way Apple frames on-device AI), translation, and a moderator copilot — all pointed at the community's own Ollama endpoint or in-browser WebLLM, with a visible "where did this inference run?" provenance badge and per-room no-ai/local-only PROP flags. Substrate: vault (local decrypted history), OroWasm host (broker), IndexedDB + sqlite-vec-class vectors.

E. Interop & ecosystem

  1. Bridge Without Becoming Them — keep one foot in Discord/Matrix/IRC while you migrate — bridges as first-class OroWasm plugins with an explicit net:outbound capability (filling the void left by dormant matterbridge), plus a Discord-compatible incoming-webhook endpoint so existing CI/RSS/GitHub integrations work by swapping one URL.
  2. The Plugin/App Platform (Yorishiro) — a real extension economy on an open protocol — the OroWasm host (capability-gated, fuel-metered pure-Zig wasm32 interpreter that already exposes plugins as real IRC commands) formalized as a versioned WIT-described ABI with published resource budgets, a signed mirrorable Git registry (Zed/HACS model), per-version malware rescanning, and a 90/10-or-better creator split.
  3. Drive the Next Generation of Standards — incubate orochi/* vendor caps, then bring field-proven PRs to IRCv3 — the WG's own proven path. The biggest open gaps (E2EE for IRC, a generalized channel-ACL, standardized webpush, moderation/observability specs) are exactly where Orochi already has running code (IRCX ACCESS ladders, WARD, Event Spine, RFC-8291 webpush).

F. Governance & community

  1. Composable, Portable Moderation (Bluesky-Ozone for chat) [new] — communities subscribe to shared, stackable moderation labelers instead of accepting one central policy — advisory-first, human-in-the-loop, never inside E2EE payloads (which is also the clean answer to EU Chat Control).
  2. Portable Reputation & Web-of-Trust [new] — reputation you carry between communities, computed from signed attestations, not owned by a platform — petname systems + signed vouches over the identity layer.
  3. Anti-Abuse Without Surveillance — layered, transparent, in-protocol — the existing WARD/flood-guard/clone-detect/DNSBL/spamtrap stack, plus proof-of-work or delay on suspect connects, exposed to operators as structured Event-Spine security events rather than a black box.

3.2 Cross-era sequencing logic

  • Era I (Torii → Washi, months 0–9): "Come for the tool." The single-tenant product must be better than Discord for one community before federation matters (Chris Dixon's law). This era is mostly client + packaging + brand — low crypto/mesh risk, fastest user-visible wins, and it ships the large body of TLS/kTLS work already landed-but-not-deployed. Exit criterion: a self-hoster runs ./orochi serve, gets a working web client in 60 seconds, imports a Discord server in an afternoon, and it feels crafted.
  • Era II (Kintsugi → Ryūjin, months 6–24): "The fortress." E2EE-everywhere + mesh hardening + leaderless federation. This is the hardest, highest-value, highest-risk crypto/distributed work — gated behind the Deterministic Ocean and (Kintsugi) an external audit. Exit criterion: two strangers on different self-hosted nodes have a post-quantum end-to-end-encrypted call, and neither operator can read it or silently swap their keys.
  • Era III (Kagura → Amanogawa, months 12–36): "The venue." Media excellence, the app ecosystem, and sovereign AI — the growth-and-retention engines. Exit criterion: a 64-seat E2EE video room with spatial audio and watch-together; a third-party plugin economy; catch-up AI that runs on the admin's GPU.
  • Era IV (Hyōryū → Yamata, months 24–48+): "Planetary." Scale, roaming, and the standards/sovereignty endgame. Exit criterion: a geo-distributed mesh serving a real institution, driving open specs the whole ecosystem adopts.

Every version lands something user-visible on its own; nothing is a pure infrastructure release that a user can't feel.

v1.1 — Sumi-e: The Time-Native Client

Promote the client's already-built primitives (vault, ?at=, heatline) from features into a paradigm.

  • Named conversations inside rooms (Zulip's proven topic model with softer on-ramps: auto-suggested names, allow drift, one-tap "split into its own thread") + topic-aware unreads — the single biggest "better than Discord" structural claim, and long-lived topics project into a browsable forum view of the same data (kills the "chat history is where knowledge dies" problem).
  • Reader mode — a typographic single-pane transcript for the ~90% who lurk; the reading persona treated as primary.
  • Time scrubber — per-room timeline strip with activity density (from chanstats hours[]), jump-to-date, shareable ?at= "moments," and a rendered "since you left" digest.
  • Calm notifications — followed-conversations + mentions badge; everything else accumulates silently into Home; a shipped Calm Mode (calm/regular/power presets); quiet Boosts (reactions that notify no one).
  • Presence as place — room header shows facepile + heatline + a visible voice "corner"; presence is peripheral, never a summons.

Why it matters: this is the differentiation no incumbent can copy cheaply, because Discord's business is engagement and Slack's is enterprise triage. Effort: M–L, pure client. See Part V for the full interface direction.

v1.2 — Washi: Atmosphere & Access

Part V — Design direction: interface, color, atmosphere

This is the deliberate, opinionated design north-star the Onyx client should adopt — grounded in the real theme factory (onyx/src/theme/paletteFactory.ts, OKLCH, the banned indigo→magenta hue band 258–342°, the Ink/Stone/Lapis/Gold/Shu-vermilion/Washi token system with AA-by-construction) and the real background engine (onyx/src/backgrounds/engine.ts, Canvas2D with an FPS-guard quality ladder, visibility pausing, and staticMode reduced-motion).

5.1 The interface paradigm — "The Time-Native Venue"

Thesis: stop designing a place to post; design a place to return to — a venue with a memory, not a message firehose with sidebars. Discord is an engagement machine wearing a chat client; Slack is enterprise triage; Zulip has the right information model in a dated body; Campfire is calm but amnesiac. The open, unoccupied position is the client that combines Zulip's conversation model, Linear/Cron's command-and-keyboard spine, Campfire/Calm-Tech's attention ethics, Gather's presence-as-place ontology, and a Figma-grade navigable timeline in one crafted body — and Onyx already has half the hard primitives (local vault, ?at= time links, presence heatlines, OKLCH theme factory).

The lessons the incumbents paid for (which we take for free): - Slack 2023 — adding a navigation layer (rail → view → channel) to fix clutter made it worse; only 17% loved it. Don't stack rails. - Discord 2025 — flattening desktop into mobile + letting monetization surfaces colonize navigation produced the loudest backlash in its history. Don't flatten; project one model at many densities. - Arc → Dia — Arc paid a "novelty tax"; distinctiveness must live in polish and atmosphere, not re-learned interaction models. Keep the Discord-shaped mental model; make everything inside it more beautiful.

The structural changes (ranked by impact):

  1. Catch-up Home as the landing surface (Zulip inbox × Slack "Catch Up") — answers "what needs me?" first; changes the daily loop for 100% of users, lurkers most of all. Arrival mode and presence mode are different modes and deserve different UIs.
  2. Named conversations inside rooms with topic-aware unreads (Zulip's model, softer on-ramps) — the information-model fix everything depends on; long-lived topics project into a forum view so knowledge stops dying in scrollback.
  3. Universal Cmd-K with a time grammar (goto #chan, at: yesterday 21:00, dm @nick, mute 1h) — collapses navigation, search, actions, and time-travel into one learnable, discoverable surface that teaches its own shortcuts (Superhuman rule). Casual users never need it; power users live in it. (onyx/src/chat/spotlight is the seed.)
  4. Time as first-class UI — per-room timeline scrubber with activity density, jump-to-date, shareable ?at= moments, "since you left" digest. The differentiator no incumbent can copy cheaply; the one place a theatrical View-Transition is warranted.
  5. Calm notification defaults + quiet reactions (Boosts) — the attention ethic that makes the product trustworthy and is structurally impossible for Discord.
  6. One-canvas responsive projection — Reader / Standard / Dense as density projections of one model; mobile is the same model, a different projection (never a separate IA — the Discord-2025 failure class, permanently avoided).
  7. Presence-as-place room header — facepile + heatline + visible voice corner; ambient, peripheral, never a summons.
  8. Reader mode — a typographic transcript for the 90% who lurk.
  9. Full keyboard layer — single-key reading nav (n next unread, j/k, u follow) wrapped in Linear-grade polish.
  10. Token-bounded community identity — a community sets accent hue, surface tint, banner, wordmark within contrast-locked OKLCH tokens (hue is free, legibility is locked). Expressive communities, one consistent product — never Revolt-style unbounded CSS.
  11. View-transition spatial motion — motion encodes location (rooms slide laterally, overlays rise, time-travel cross-fades with a date stamp); the calmest animation in the whole app is an incoming message (never engagement-bait kinetics). The Family/Rauno Freiberg/Emil Kowalski school; the View Transitions API is Baseline in 2026.
  12. Forum projection of long-lived conversations — durable content gets a durable surface.

Explicitly reject: Slack's extra-rail indirection; Discord's mobile-desktop flattening; quests/streaks/orbs and all engagement kinetics; unbounded per-community CSS; the AI-chatbot-as-front-door (LLM features live inside the palette and digest, never as the interface); notification-default culture; sidebar taxonomies more than two levels deep.

5.2 The color system — "Ink & Vermillion" on a scientific OKLCH factory

Why the identity is well-timed. Vermilion (Japanese shu 朱 — the cinnabar red-orange of torii gates and the inkan seal) carries a millennium of "ink + vermillion" pairing: black sumi text, one vermillion seal as the singular mark of identity. It is culturally coherent with the codebase's own naming (Orochi, Suimyaku, Tsumugi, Tegami, Kagura), and the 2025–26 color zeitgeist is explicitly a revolt against interchangeable SaaS blue/purple (Pantone 2025 Mocha Mousse, 2026 Cloud Dancer — warm neutrals, organic off-whites, one bold accent). Ink & Vermillion is the cleanest possible anti-Discord-blurple position, and the theme factory's existing banned indigo→magenta band (258–342°) already enforces not being Discord.

Adopt a three-layer token architecture (Radix/Material/Carbon consensus): factory-generated 12-step OKLCH primitives per hue → semantic tokens (--bg-app/surface/raised, --comp{,-hover,-active}, --border-*, --text-1/2/3, --accent-solid{,-hover}, --on-accent, --accent-text, status) → component tokens only where components diverge. Because every hue ramp shares one fixed lightness skeleton and the factory solves steps 9/11/12 for contrast targets (Adobe-Leonardo style) rather than tabulating them, any of the 14 hues "just works" by construction — the existing enforceAA is already this discipline; extend it to APCA targets with WCAG 2.2 as the compliance floor.

Flagship "Ink & Vermillion" spec (dark "Ink"): warm near-black grounds (oklch(0.16 0.010 55) → not gray), warm paper-white text (--washi ≈ oklch(0.93 0.012 80), Lc 90+), vermillion accent-solid oklch(0.62 0.205 30) used scarcely (a seal, not a paint bucket), accent-text oklch(0.76 0.14 34) for links/mentions, mention wash as accent-at-14%-alpha + a 2px solid rail. Light "Paper": Cloud-Dancer-warm oklch(0.977 0.006 85) grounds (never #fff), warm near-black ink text, accent-solid dropped to oklch(0.58 0.205 29) to clear AA with white labels (the classic orange-button gap, honestly handled). P3 enhancement: boost accent chroma to 0.232 under @media (color-gamut: p3) only — vermillion is exactly the hue where P3 adds visible fire.

The chat-specific fixes (top color moves): 1. Warm the ink — move all neutrals from gray to warm-tinted (H≈55–85, C 0.008–0.015); the single cheapest "luxury, not template" signal. 2. Nick/role hue-lock algorithm — hash the nick to hue only (fnv1a → 11 buckets excluding the vermillion band), with L and C as pre-verified theme constants (alternating L across buckets aids color-blind distinction). Every nick color passes contrast in every theme by construction — the structural fix Discord never shipped (no single hex can be legible on both light and dark, which is why Discord role colors are a years-old complaint). Roles store a hue, not a hex. 3. Enforce a chroma budget — neutrals ≥90% of pixels at C≤0.02; nick/role mid-chroma; only accent + status hit high chroma; never more than one high-chroma element per message row (the antidote to both "all one hue" monotony and rainbow incoherence). 4. Mentions as container color (wash + rail), never text color — legibility becomes independent of semantics. 5. Shape-code status (disc/crescent/bar/ring) + per-mode OKLCH tuning; shift DND to crimson (oklch(0.60 0.19 12)) so it doesn't collide with the vermillion brand. 6. light-dark() + relative-color derivation for all state colors (halves the token surface); prefers-contrast: more overlay + forced-colors pass; contrast-color() as the on-accent safety net (Baseline 2026).

Making 14 themes one family, not 14 skins: identity lives in structure (one lightness skeleton, one chroma-curve shape, all neutrals tinted 1–2% toward the theme hue, one semantic contract, brand constants — the seal stays vermillion, status hues stay fixed, focus behavior stays fixed). Ink & Vermillion is the flagship instance; every other theme is the same instrument in a different key.

5.3 The background system — "Ink on Living Paper"

The engine (onyx/src/backgrounds/engine.ts) is already ahead of most products — rAF discipline, visibility pause, FPS-guard quality ladder, staticMode freeze-the-theme's-own-scene reduced-motion. This builds on it, not over it.

Standardize on one layered ambient stack (the 22 variants become presets of it): 1. Ground — theme-derived, luminance-capped (oklch L ≤ 0.32 dark / ≥ 0.88 light). This cap is the legibility contract: text contrast holds at every frame by construction, no per-pixel scrim needed. 2. Ink layer — the current Canvas2D scene (blooms, currents, veins, rain) in theme tokens at low alpha, composed so the message column is the calmest zone and energy lives at the edges. Keep Canvas2D (WebGL buys nothing at these fill rates); vendor a ~10 KB mesh-gradient only if a true gradient variant is wanted. 3. Washi grain — one static feTurbulence tile at 4–6% soft-light, identical across all 14 themes — the brand signature material ("ink needs paper"); never animated. Grain is the single highest-leverage anti-generic move of the era — it's precisely what mass-produced AI-gradient UIs lack. 4. Vignette — static radial darkening behind the message column. 5. Kintsugi accent — the one permitted vermillion/gold element: a single slow seam or bloom per viewport — the screenshotable brand move that scarcity everywhere else earns.

Reactivity: channel accent → hue-rotate the same scene (Arc Spaces model — per-community identity, zero new assets); motion-on-message-send (the Telegram move — a bloom breathes when you send; motion as feedback, not ambience; highest delight, lowest risk); voice-RMS (heavily low-passed) modulates bloom breathing ±20% (rooms feel alive because of people); slow time-of-day warmth drift.

Guardrails (deltas to the engine): cap ambient frame rate at ~30 fps (halves steady-state power for an all-day app); idle deceleration to a hold-frame after ~60s (the biggest total-energy win); window-blur pause; an explicit in-app Animated / Still / Off control (WCAG 2.2.2, which prefers-reduced-motion alone doesn't satisfy); honor prefers-reduced-transparency (translucent shell surfaces go opaque); enforce the OKLCH luminance cap in variants/utils.ts with a unit test asserting each variant's brightest frame stays under the ceiling.

Signature scene set (~5): Sumi Drift (default — ink blooms + rain on washi grain, send-breath), Kintsugi (dark ground, one gold-vermillion seam — the postcard), Deep Current (cool themes), Ember Field (warm, voice-reactive), Washi Still (grain + vignette only — doubles as the reduced-motion/battery/Still rendering of every theme).

Part VII — Adoption, sustainability & governance

7.1 The adoption playbook (sequenced)

0 → 1K users (Torii era): self-hosters + dogfood. Moves: one-binary 60-second quickstart; one-click deploy everywhere; rich invites; serial Show HN launches of the genuinely novel internals (CRDT mesh, zero-drop USR2 upgrades, the custom PQ TLS stack, Deterministic Ocean) — each is HN-catnip and funnels a spike to the live flagship mesh. Never demo an empty network — the flagship mesh (eshmaki.me + ircx.us) is the product; "join #orochi in your browser, then docker run your own node and it's meshed with us in 60 seconds" is a demo no competitor can match (Revolt can't federate, Matrix takes an afternoon).

1K → 100K (Ryūjin era): FOSS anchor tenants + migration tooling + event-readiness. Personal-founder outreach (the Discord/FFXIV move) to ~10 flagship FOSS projects with live grievances (Slack-history deletion, Discord ToS/age-ID anxiety) — offer free flagship hosting + white-glove Discord import + a Libera bridge + public searchable archives with permalink URLs. FOSS projects migrate as units (they did it in 30 days in 2021) and each brings 100s–1000s of members. Ship prewritten "Leaving X? Here's your community in 10 minutes" pages within 24h of predictable shocks (Discord age-verification/breaches, country bans, Slack pricing changes) — Telegram gained 25M in 72h by being ready.

100K → 1M (venue era): creators (0% platform take vs Discord's 90/10 — "keep 100%") + fandom + the EU institutional track (slow, procurement-heavy, high-ARPU). Retention loops mature (recurring events + countdown + reminders; default-quiet notifications + webpush "come back" delivery; presence heatline; auto-archive dead channels) — the difference between wave-acquisition and a rising waterline vs. the Bluesky/Mastodon 40–70% collapse.

Target-vertical order: self-hosters → FOSS → privacy/crypto → creators → fandom → EU public sector (parallel) → gaming edges only (banned regions, retro/emu/modding) → mutual-aid as goodwill.

7.2 Sustainability (the anti-enshittification constitution)

  • License: keep AGPLv3, full-featured, no open-core feature gates (the Ghost/Zulip rule — everything the protocol needs is free; sell convenience, accountability, identity, never capability). The 2024–26 evidence is decisive: the SSPL/BSL rugpull era failed and reversed (Elastic and Redis both returned to AGPL); Element relicensing Synapse to AGPLv3 is the on-point precedent. Keep the protocol spec + client libs permissive (MPL/Apache) for third-party clients. Register the trademark — the name, the flagship network, and release velocity are the real moat; AGPL is the fence. Use DCO not CLA, and keep copyright consolidated so a dual-license enterprise option stays available without CLA friction.
  • Revenue stack (ranked): (1) managed node hosting ("your mesh node in 5 minutes, custom domain, backups, upgrades" — the Discourse/Ghost/Plausible engine, slow but the only proven path to multi-million ARR while the software stays 100% free); (2) government/sovereign deployments + support SLAs (highest ARPU, riding the EU wave; AGPL-exception dual-license for integrators who refuse copyleft); (3) flagship premium accounts + cosmetics (the Matrix.org freemium template + Discord's lesson that identity cosmetics are the most ethics-compatible network revenue — themes/effects/vanity handles, never functional gating, never on self-hosted nodes); (4) grants (Sovereign Tech Fund fits "critical comms infrastructure"; NSF SBIR; OpenSats as freedom-tech — treat as runway for audits/hardening, not the business); (5) donations (Coolify-scale at best; never payroll).
  • Endgame structure: land in a foundation-owned shape (Ghost/Zulip-2026 model) before revenue makes it contentious — the failure modes on record (Mattermost tier clawbacks, Rocket.Chat EE creep, MinIO console-gutting, HashiCorp BSL→IBM) all trace to investor/exit pressure a foundation forecloses.
  • Flagship economics honesty: budget moderation ≥ infrastructure from day one (Matrix's 30% vs 20%); cap free-tier media (the exact Matrix lever); form the cheap legal entity early (OSA/DSA/Chat-Control regulation is now a real cost line); keep the flagship deliberately non-dominant in the mesh (encourage institutional nodes early) so its cost and regulatory exposure stay bounded.

7.3 Governance & anti-abuse

  • Composable, portable moderation (Bluesky-Ozone model): communities subscribe to shared, stackable labelers; advisory-first, human-in-the-loop (5–7% ML false-positive rates make auto-punishment toxic); never inside E2EE payloads — which is simultaneously the design answer and the regulatory answer to EU Chat Control (moderation without scanning).
  • Verifiable moderation (ProofMark): signed, auditable actions via the Event Spine + WARD; users get a redacted proof id, opers get the chain.
  • Anti-abuse without surveillance: the existing layered stack (WARD, flood-guard, clone-detect, DNSBL, spamtrap, +j raid-guard) + optional proof-of-work/delay on suspect connects, all surfaced as structured Event-Spine security events — anti-abuse as a config block, not tribal knowledge.
  • Trust engineering as brand: publish the Deterministic Ocean design + live fuzz counters + a seed→bug→one-command-replay demo; simulate USR2-under-fault (the Jepsen/TigerBeetle finding that upgrades are where panics live); run the daemon through Antithesis's OSS program; publish chat-specific SLOs and honest postmortems. "The most tested chat server in the world" is a checkable, currently-uncontested claim.

Appendix A — Brand architecture recommendation

The project currently sprawls across Orochi (daemon), Onyx (client), IRCXNet (network), eshmaki.me (site), plus dead names (Ocean, Ruri) — a four-way Matrix-style split with zero marketing behind any name, and a collision risk (Discord's own darkest 2025 theme is literally named "Onyx"). The 2024–26 evidence (Matrix/Element/Synapse confusion vs. Bluesky's clean one-brand/app.bsky.*-namespace model; Snikket's "products win users, protocols win ecosystems") points to a branded house:

  • One consumer-facing master brand — the name a user says in "join me on ___." Because chat brands are network brands (people join places, not binaries), the network/community identity is the natural master brand.
  • Orochi = the engine (devs and self-hosters love an engine name — like Synapse, but never shown to end users). The client app is presented under the master brand; Onyx survives as an internal codename and/or a theme name.
  • Protocol namespaced under the brand, Bluesky-style — the orochi/* vendor-cap namespace already exists, so this is nearly free.
  • Retire "IRCXNet" as a public identity — protocol-acronym names (XMPP, ActivityPub) are proven adoption liabilities, and "IRC" imports 30 years of "hard to use" perception the whole roadmap is built to disprove. Keep IRC compatibility as a feature bullet ("works with any IRC client"), never the identity.
  • "Ink & Vermillion" = the visual identity/design language, not a product name — strong, distinctive, culturally coherent with the Japanese-named internals.
  • If a rename happens, use Ergo's playbook (announce early, repos with redirects, "formerly X" for a year, make migration boring). Enforce one glossary (rooms/channels/spaces — pick and hold) across site, client, docs, invite unfurls, and server MOTD (Snikket's rule).
